Ready · what is a landing zone?

An Azure landing zone has two parts

🏛️ Platform landing zone

  • The centralized foundation: governance, security and shared resources for all workloads.
  • Managed by the platform team.
  • Usually just one per Microsoft Entra tenant.

⚓ Workload landing zones

  • Where workload teams deploy and run their workloads, inside the platform's guardrails.
  • Managed by workload teams.
  • Learn renamed these from "application landing zones" (October 2026).
Learn's definition: "a proven and flexible architecture for governing, securing, and scaling" a multi-subscription Azure environment.
Sources on Microsoft Learn: What is an Azure landing zone? (updated 2026-09-30) · What's new in Microsoft's Cloud Adoption Framework (updated 2026-10-30)

Resource organization starts with management groups: containers for subscriptions, arranged as a tree.

Animated architecture (text version)

Management groups and policy inheritance

  1. An initiative is assigned at the intermediate root, Contoso. Example: the built-in "Allowed locations" policy with a deny effect.
  2. The assignment cascades by inheritance to every child management group...
  3. ...and on down to every subscription and resource below them.
  4. Corp: needs the corporate network through the hub. Online: may need direct internet traffic. Local: Azure Local clusters.
  5. Sandbox: isolated from Corp and Online, with a less restrictive set of policies.
  6. Decommissioned: canceled landing zones wait here. Azure deletes them after 30–60 days.

✎ Learn's newest page groups Corp ("Internal"), Online and Local under "Workload landing zones"; older pages say "Landing zones".

Sources on Microsoft Learn: Management groups (Azure landing zone design area) (updated 2025-12-19) · What is an Azure landing zone? (updated 2026-09-30) · What are Azure management groups? (updated 2025-07-21) · Azure Policy definitions effect basics (updated 2025-03-04)

DewiSo a policy assigned near the top flows down to every subscription below it?

BayuYes. Learn: governance applied to a management group cascades by inheritance to all its subscriptions.

SoraAnd it's an explicit deny system. You can't loosen a parent's deny with a softer policy lower down. You exclude the child scope instead.

Checkpoint: A policy with a deny effect is assigned at the Contoso management group. A team wants a softer rule in its own subscription below. What does Learn say?
  • Assign a looser policy at the subscription. The lowest scope wins
  • Azure Policy is an explicit deny system. Exclude the child scope instead of assigning a looser policy
  • Move the subscription to the tenant root group

Answer: Azure Policy is an explicit deny system. Exclude the child scope instead of assigning a looser policy. Assignments cascade down the tree, and a looser child assignment can't override a parent's deny.
