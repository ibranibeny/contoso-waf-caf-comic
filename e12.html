Cost Optimization: five design principles

  1. 1
    Develop cost-management disciplineThe whole team knows the budget, the spending and the reports.
  2. 2
    Design with a cost-efficiency mindsetSpend only on what returns value.
  3. 3
    Design for usage optimizationGet full use out of what you already pay for.
  4. 4
    Design for rate optimizationPay a lower rate for the same thing, without redesigning.
  5. 5
    Monitor and optimize over timeKeep right-sizing as the workload evolves.

Animated architecture (text version)

The cost loop

  1. Usage and cost data flow into Microsoft Cost Management.
  2. A budget compares actual and forecasted cost with its thresholds (up to five).
  3. A threshold is crossed: the cost alert notifies the team. The resources keep running.
  4. Azure Advisor spots an underutilized VM: right-size it, or shut it down.
  5. Stable, always-on usage? A reservation lowers the rate.
  6. Dynamic or evolving usage? A savings plan. Right-size first: discounts reduce rates, not waste.
Sources on Microsoft Learn: Tutorial: Create and manage budgets (updated 2025-06-26) · Use cost alerts to monitor usage and spending (updated 2025-06-26) · Optimize VM or VMSS spend by resizing or shutting down underutilized instances (updated 2026-02-18) · Decide between a savings plan and a reservation (updated 2026-10-29)

Checkpoint: A Cost Management budget reaches 100% of its amount. What happens to the resources?
  • They're stopped automatically
  • Nothing. The budget sends alerts, and the resources keep running
  • They move to a cheaper region

Answer: Nothing. The budget sends alerts, and the resources keep running. Learn: "Resources aren't affected, and your consumption isn't stopped." Someone, or automation, has to act on the alert.
