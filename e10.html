Reliability: five design principles

  1. 1
    Design for business requirementsSet clear, documented goals, negotiated with business stakeholders.
  2. 2
    Design for resilienceKeep working, fully or in a reduced mode, when parts fail.
  3. 3
    Design for recoveryAnticipate failures and recover with minimal disruption.
  4. 4
    Design for operationsShift left: anticipate failure conditions, observe, learn.
  5. 5
    Keep it simpleAvoid overengineering the design, the code and the operations.

Zone redundancy: losing a zone, not the shop

  1. A normal day: Application Gateway spreads requests across App Service instances in three availability zones.
  2. Health probes: Application Gateway checks every back-end instance, and App Service health check watches them too.
  3. Zone 1 has an outage. Its instance fails the health checks and is taken out of rotation.
  4. Requests flow to the healthy instances in zones 2 and 3. Customers keep shopping.
  5. The zone-redundant database keeps serving. For zone-redundant resources, Microsoft manages zone failover automatically.
  6. Zone 1 recovers and its instance rejoins. Learn's advice: overprovision so the remaining zones can carry the load.

✎ A single-region design. Surviving the loss of a whole region needs a multi-region design.

DewiAnd what does all this cost us?

SoraMore replicas mean more cost, and more surface area to secure. Replication can add latency. Reliability is never free, so we match it to the target.

Checkpoint: Zone 1 fails. The shop runs App Service instances in zones 1, 2 and 3 behind a zone-redundant Application Gateway. What happens?
  • Health probes find the unhealthy instance and traffic flows to zones 2 and 3
  • The whole shop is down until zone 1 recovers
  • Traffic automatically moves to another Azure region

Answer: Health probes find the unhealthy instance and traffic flows to zones 2 and 3. Zone redundancy keeps capacity in other zones, and health probes route around unhealthy instances. Surviving a whole-region outage needs a multi-region design.
